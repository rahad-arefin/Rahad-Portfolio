The first 90 days as Head of Digital: a practical playbook

How to spend your first 90 days as Head of Digital: listen and audit, deliver quick wins, then build the operating system. What to avoid in month one.

THE SHORT ANSWER

Spend the first 30 days listening and auditing, the next 30 delivering quick wins that build trust, and the last 30 building the operating system: a testing roadmap, team rituals, a hiring plan and automation. Avoid rebrands, agency changes and big budget increases in month one, but fix broken tracking and obvious waste immediately.

The first 90 days decide how much trust a new digital leader gets for the next two years. Move too fast and you break things you did not understand. Move too slowly and the business stops expecting change. This is the plan I use, built from leading a 24-person team and advising business owners across five countries.

Days 1 to 30: listen and audit

The goal of month one is an accurate picture, not visible activity.

  • One-to-ones: the CEO, sales, product, finance and every person on the team. Ask each one what is working, what is broken and what they would change first.
  • Accounts and tracking: audit every ad account, analytics setup and CRM. I use a 90-minute audit routine per account.
  • Unit economics: customer acquisition cost, payback period, lifetime value and marketing efficiency ratio. If nobody can answer these, that is your first finding.
  • Team skills map: who is strong at what, where the gaps are, and who is overloaded.

Gate: a written diagnosis and three priorities agreed with the CEO.

What I do not do in month one

  • No rebrand, however tempting the old brand looks.
  • No agency or vendor changes before I understand why they were chosen.
  • No big budget increases before measurement is trustworthy.
  • No restructuring of the team before I have seen people work.

Two exceptions: broken tracking and obvious wasted spend get fixed immediately. Waiting on those costs real money every day.

Days 31 to 60: quick wins that build trust

  • Fix the tracking gaps the audit found.
  • Cut wasted spend and reallocate budget on an 80/20 model.
  • Launch one dashboard leadership trusts, built on pipeline metrics such as cost per SAL rather than clicks.
  • Speed up lead handling: automatic routing and fast follow-up protect every ad dollar already spent.

Gate: a trusted dashboard is live and the first waste is cut.

Days 61 to 90: build the engine

  • Testing roadmap: a quarterly plan of experiments with hypotheses, budgets and kill rules.
  • Team rituals: weekly pod syncs, monthly performance reviews and clear ownership.
  • Hiring plan: the roles the plan needs, in the order it needs them.
  • Automation: reporting and lead handling, so the team spends time on decisions. My zero-cost AI reporting setup is one example.
  • Targets: next-quarter goals agreed with the CEO, not handed down.

Gate: the quarterly plan is signed off.

Deliverables by phase

Reporting to the CEO

Every week, one page: what changed, what we learned, what is next, and the handful of numbers that matter. Short, honest updates build more trust than polished monthly decks, especially when the news is mixed.

Key takeaways

  • Month one is for an accurate picture, not visible activity.
  • Fix broken tracking and obvious waste immediately; wait on everything else.
  • Quick wins in month two earn the trust to build in month three.
  • End each phase at a gate the CEO can see.

Frequently asked questions

What should a new Head of Digital do in the first week?

Meet the CEO, sales, product, finance and every team member one to one, get read access to every account and tool, and ask each person what they think is broken. Do not announce changes yet.

What should a new Head of Digital avoid in the first 30 days?

Rebranding, replacing agencies, restructuring the team and raising budgets. The exceptions are broken tracking and obvious wasted spend, which should be fixed straight away.

How should progress be reported to the CEO?

A one-page weekly update: what changed, what we learned, what is next, and the few numbers that matter, such as cost per qualified lead and pipeline created.
